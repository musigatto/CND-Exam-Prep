# Risk Context, Identification, Assessment, Analysis, Levels, Matrix (LO#02)

> **LO#02: Learn to manage risk through risk management program** _(Mod 18 pp12–18)_
> This section explains risk management phases in an organization's risk management program _(Mod 18 p12)_. This note covers establishing context, identification, assessment, analysis, levels, and the matrix; treatment and plan follow in the sibling note.

## Establishing context

- **Understand the current posture** the organization operates in _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Define the **external and internal environment** in which the organization is operating _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Establishing Context step: the employee **defines the external and internal environment** and understands the **current conditions** in which the organization operates _(Mod 18 p13)_.

## Risk identification — foundation and first step

- **Foundation and first step** of risk management; lists **risks and their characteristics before such risks harm** the organization; depends on the **skill set of individuals** and differs by organization _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Identifies the **sources, causes, and consequences** of all **internal and external risks** affecting organizational security _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Identified risks are **recorded in a risk register** and further analyzed; identification is an **iterative process** _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Purpose: generate a list of **threats and opportunities** based on risk events that respectively **prevent and enhance** the achievement of objectives _(Mod 18 p13)_.
- Role groupings _(Mod 18 p13)_:
  - **Environment**: crowded workspaces, clutter, hot/cold environments, smoking, poor lighting, electrical hazards.
  - **Equipment**: poor condition, non-functioning devices, unavailability, task-inappropriate equipment.
  - **Client**: conditions changing, unpredictable movements, poor communication.
  - **Tasks**: insufficient time allocated, repetitive tasks, work design, task organization, fixed posture, poor postures, insufficient employee numbers.

## Main elements in risk identification

| Element | Printed meaning |
|---|---|
| Description / Event | An occurrence or a particular set of circumstances _(Mod 18 p14)_ |
| Causes | Factors that may contribute to a risk occurring _(Mod 18 p14)_ |
| Consequences | Impact of an event _(Mod 18 p14)_ |

- **Quantifying Risks**: determines the **impact of risk** and **calibrates the possible outcome** of the risks _(Mod 18 pp13–14)_.
- Priorities: know **what to consider** so major issues are not missed; gather information from **multiple sources**; discuss **old, current, and evolving issues, data analysis, review of performance indicators, data loss, and scenario planning** with stakeholders _(Mod 18 p14)_.
- Techniques: **checklists, flow charts, and systems analysis** _(Mod 18 p14)_.
- Document _(Mod 18 p14)_: risk description; **how and why** the risk occurs; **existing internal controls** that may mitigate likelihood or consequences; **methods** that identify the risks; **scope** covered; **participants**; **information sources consulted**.
- Analyze the **risk identification process's effectiveness** _(Mod 18 p14)_.

## Risk assessment

- Assesses the organization's risk; provides an **estimate on the likelihood and impact** of the risk _(Mod 18 p15)_.
- An **ongoing iterative process** assigning priorities to **risk mitigation and implementation plans**; determines the **quantitative and qualitative value** of risk _(Mod 18 p15)_.
- Determines the **types of risks**, **likelihood and severity**, and **priorities and plans for risk control**; performed when a hazard is identified but **cannot be controlled immediately** _(Mod 18 p15)_.
- After assessment, all **information facilities should be updated at regular intervals**; assessed risks are **prioritized by severity or impact**; the prioritized list drives **task sequence** and **resource allocation** _(Mod 18 p15)_.
- Severity priorities _(Mod 18 p15)_:
  - **1–2**: eliminate **immediately (usually within 24 hours)**; if elimination is impossible, reduce to a lower rating with **at least one control measure**.
  - **3–4**: eliminate or control **within a reasonable timeframe**.
  - **5–6**: eliminate **as soon as possible** or control the hazard **when possible**.

## Steps in risk assessment: analysis and prioritization

- **Risk Analysis**: analyzes the risk of **vulnerabilities and threats** to provide an understanding of the **inherent and controlled risks**; defines the **nature of the risk**; determines the **level of risk exposure** _(Mod 18 pp15–16)_.
- **Risk Prioritization**: prioritize and rate risks of the **same severity** so an appropriate response plan may be designed; depends on the **goals and resources** of the organization _(Mod 18 p16)_.
- Consider for prioritization _(Mod 18 p16)_:
  - **Immediate and future impact** on goals, assets, other organizations, and the nation.
  - **Expected loss** because of a risk.
  - **Relationship** of a risk and/or mitigation to other risks and/or mitigations.
  - **Managing the impact of threats** from a risk.
- Slide triad: risks are **prioritized and treated according to severity**; consider prioritization while performing the **risk response** step _(Mod 18 p15)_.

## Risk levels and actions (Table 18.1)

- Risks are categorized into **high, medium, and low** (table prints **Extreme/High, Medium, Low**) according to **estimated impact on the system**; impact depends on the **value of assets and resources** affected and the **severity of the damage** _(Mod 18 p17)_.
- Levels present the actions staff should take _(Mod 18 p17)_:

| Risk level | Action (prose) |
|---|---|
| Extreme / High | Immediate measures: **isolate, eliminate, and substitute** the risk through effective risk controls _(Mod 18 p17)_ |
| Medium (upper) | Identify and impose controls and **define strict timelines** to reduce risk to a reasonably lower level, though the existing system **can continue to operate** _(Mod 18 p17)_ |
| Medium (lower) | **Stop the activity unless the risk is reduced** to a low or medium level _(Mod 18 p17)_ |
| Low (upper) | Immediate action not required, but measures **implemented quickly**; implement controls **as soon as possible** to reduce risk to a reasonably lower level _(Mod 18 p17)_ |
| Low (lower) | Take **preventive steps** to mitigate the impact; **ignore** as they generally pose no significant problem, with **periodical review** to keep controls effective _(Mod 18 p17)_ |

- Slide-condensed wording for the same levels: **immediate measures to combat risk; identify and impose controls to reduce risk to a reasonably low level; immediate action not required but implemented quickly; implement controls as soon as possible; take preventive steps** _(Mod 18 p17)_.

## Risk matrix

- A **risk matrix scales risk** by considering the **probability, likelihood, and consequence/impact** of the risk _(Mod 18 p18)_.
- The risk assessment matrix identifies the **probability of failure and high-risk areas**; a risk-level matrix must be developed to **measure or assess** a risk _(Mod 18 p18)_.
- Formula: **Risk rating = Probability (Likelihood) x Severity**, where Probability measures the **likelihood that an uncertain event will occur**, and severity is the **degree of impact of damage** caused by it _(Mod 18 p18)_.
- Severity classes: **severe, major, moderate, minor, insignificant** _(Mod 18 p18)_.
- Probability bands (rows) vs consequences (columns), tile values as printed _(Mod 18 p18)_:

| Probability | Insignificant | Minor | Moderate | Major | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 81–100% | Low | Medium | High | Extreme | Extreme |
| 61–80% | Low | Medium | High | High | Extreme |
| 41–60% | Low | Medium | Medium | High | High |
| 21–40% | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| 1–20% | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |

- Priority of an event is classified into **five categories** and mapped against **severity and probability** _(Mod 18 p18)_.
- Features of a risk matrix _(Mod 18 p18)_: **quantitative/semi-quantitative** hazard analysis tool; **simple and easy to understand**; **clearly defined tolerable and non-tolerable ranges** before developing it; **detailed descriptions of all consequences**; uses **orders of magnitude and constant likelihood range** to cover total risks; good **qualitative** guidance that may not need prior knowledge; **flexible** for different organization-specific risk targets; **no prior software knowledge required**, executable using software.
