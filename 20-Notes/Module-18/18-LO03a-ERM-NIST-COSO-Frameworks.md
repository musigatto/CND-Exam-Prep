# ERM, NIST RMF and COSO ERM (LO#03, pp25–33)

> **LO#03: Learn different risk management frameworks (RMFs)**
> Covers pp25–33. Continued in [[18-LO03b-Governance-Policy-Vendors]] (governance, policy, vendors).

## Why RMFs _(Mod 18 p25)_

- Organizations establish an RMF to **understand the overall risk level**.
- Infrastructure and its potential risks differ per organization; **strategic objectives and stakeholder needs** determine the RMF required.
- Knowing frameworks lets the organization choose the most appropriate one.

## Enterprise network risk management _(Mod 18 p26)_

- Attempts to **identify, assess, and mitigate threats to the various assets** of an organization.
- **Systematic approach** to reach goals and objectives satisfying **business requirements**.
- Identifies **staff, tools, required skills, and other resources**; the defender must:
  1. Identify and understand the current risks.
  2. Develop procedures to combat network risks.
  3. Mitigate the various network risks.
  4. Develop risk assessment and management policies, procedures, and checklists.

## Enterprise Risk Management framework (ERM) _(Mod 18 pp27–28)_

- **Methods and processes to minimize the impact of risks**; involves **planning, organizing, leading, and controlling** organizational activities; a **risk-based approach**.
- Framework steps: **identify events** relevant to objectives (risks and opportunities); **assess likelihood and magnitude of impact**; **determine a response strategy**; **monitor**.
- Prose actions: **risk avoidance by aborting the actions that lead to risks; reducing risks by reducing likelihood or impact; standardizing the risk management process**.
- Figure contrast (prose carried): the **RMF defines implementation activities** for how an organization handles risk; the **ERM provides a structured process integrating information security and risk management activities**.
- Key activities for enterprise-level risk (from IS operation), in printed order:
  1. Classification of the information system.
  2. Selection of appropriate security controls.
  3. Refining the selected set based on the risk assessment.
  4. Documenting all selected controls in a system security plan.
  5. Implementation of the security controls.
  6. Security controls assessment.
  7. Determining agency-level risk and risk acceptability.
  8. Authorizing information system operation.
  9. Monitoring security controls on a continuous basis.
- Helps an organization understand: **risks coverage; risk appetite; risk governance (culture, governance, policies); risk data and infrastructure; risks control environment; risk measurement and evaluation; risks response**.

## Goals of the ERM framework _(Mod 18 p29)_

- Common goal and challenge: **improving capability and coordination while integrating output into a unified picture of risk for stakeholders**; improve ability to manage risks effectively.
- Figure numbers eight goals; prose adds two more — all ten carried:

| # | Goal (prose wording) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Integrate the ERM with an organization's performance management |
| 2 | Communicate the benefits of risk management |
| 3 | Define the roles and responsibilities in an organization to manage risks |
| 4 | Standardize the risk-reporting and escalating process |
| 5 | Set a standard approach to manage risks in an organization |
| 6 | Assist the resources in managing the risk |
| 7 | Set the scope and application of risk management in an organization |
| 8 | Mandate periodic reviews and verification for improvements of the ERM |
| 9 | Convey an organization's policies, approach, and attitude toward risk management |
| 10 | Ensure that an organization should meet risk-reporting commitments |

## NIST Risk Management Framework _(Mod 18 pp30–31)_

- Set of **information security policies and standards for the federal government developed by NIST**; **structured and continuous** process **integrated into the system development life cycle**; aids **early detection and resolution**; applies to **new and legacy systems**.
- Source: `https://csrc.nist.gov`
- Seven tasks in printed order:

| Task | Printed definition |
|---|---|
| Prepare | Carry out essential activities at all levels to manage security and privacy risks; outcomes are key risk management and common controls identified, organization-wide assessment done, organization risk management strategy established |
| Categorize | Categorize the system and information processed, stored, transmitted based on an impact analysis (worst-case adverse impact to mission and business functions) |
| Select | Select the set of NIST SP 800-53 controls to protect the system based on risk assessment(s); select baseline per categorization |
| Implement | Implement controls, integrate with legacy systems using sound engineering practices, apply configuration settings, document deployment and environmental impact |
| Assess | Verify controls are in place, operating as planned, producing anticipated results; evaluate effectiveness and correct working |
| Authorize | Senior official makes a risk-based decision to authorize the system (to operate), based on risk to operations, assets, individuals, other organizations, and the nation against accepted risk appetite |
| Monitor | Continuously monitor control implementation and risks; track changes and signs of attacks; regularly check effectiveness |

- Assessment sub-steps in printed order: develop the security assessment plan; determine which controls are to be assessed; select appropriate procedures; determine depth and coverage needed for assurance; tailor the procedures; finalize the plan and obtain approval; conduct the assessment; analyze the results; create the security assessment report. _(Mod 18 p31)_

## COSO ERM framework _(Mod 18 pp32–33)_

- The **Committee of Sponsoring Organizations (COSO) of the Treadway Commission**, established in the **mid-1980s** as part of the **National Commission on Fraudulent Financial Reporting**.
- Addresses ERM evolution and the need to improve risk management for an evolving business environment; **considers risk in both the strategy-setting process and driving performance**.
- Defines essential components, suggests a **common language**, gives direction and guidance; ERM covers the management-process elements enabling **genuine risk-based decisions**.
- Source: `http://www.coso.org`
- A **set of principles organized into five interrelated components**; prose details four (fifth present only as garbled figure tiles — refused):

| Component | Printed principles |
|---|---|
| Governance and Culture | Exercises Board Risk Oversight; Establishes Operating Structures; Defines Desired Culture; Demonstrates Commitment to Core Values; Attracts, Develops, and Retains Capable Individuals |
| Strategy and Objective-Setting | Analyzes Business Context; Defines Risk Appetite; Evaluates Alternative Strategies; Formulates Business Objectives (risk appetite aligned with strategy; objectives as basis for identifying, assessing, responding to risk) |
| Performance | Identifies Risk; Assesses Severity of Risk; Prioritizes Risks; Implements Risk Responses; Develops Portfolio View (prioritized by severity in context of risk appetite; portfolio view of assumed risk reported to key stakeholders) |
| Review and Revision | Review entity performance; consider how well ERM components function over time and under substantial change; decide revisions needed |

- Governance sets tone and oversight for ERM; culture covers ethical values, desired behaviors, understanding of risk.
